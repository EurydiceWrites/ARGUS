## What I built

`src/video_motion_detect.py` — a single-file CV pipeline:

1. ffmpeg full-rate frame extraction.
2. ORB + RANSAC homography to register every frame to frame 0, with a
   per-frame "HUD mask" excluding (a) cyan/green HUD overlays — reticle,
   "N" indicator, vehicle-tracking corner brackets — and (b) pure-black
   redaction rectangles. Both classes are pixel-fixed in the original
   frame and would otherwise be warped by stabilization into fake motion.
3. Per-pixel temporal median across the stabilized stack as the static
   background.
4. Per-frame foreground = `frame > dilated(median) + threshold` (and
   the symmetric dark version). The local-max/min trick gives ±3 px
   tolerance for sub-pixel registration error on bright building edges.
5. Contour filter by area + circularity (drops linear edge residuals).
6. Greedy nearest-neighbour linker into tracks; keep tracks ≥ 4 frames
   AND ≥ 30 px end-to-end displacement (a real moving object travels).
7. Outputs: `trajectory_overlay.png`, `motion_accumulator.png`,
   `foreground_accumulator.png`, `median_background.png`,
   `hud_mask_union.png`, `best_frame_crop.png`, `detections.csv`,
   `motion_summary.{json,txt}`, and an `annotated/` sequence at every
   10th frame with the dominant-track centroid circled.

### V1 (DOD_111688809) detail

The track the pipeline found:
- starts bottom-left (~21% from left, 93% from top) at frame 0,
- crosses the frame on a near-perfectly straight diagonal,
- ends mid-right (~70%, 47%) at frame 129,
- then is lost (possibly occluded or simply exits detection — second-longest
  track 62–136 may be the same object across a brief gap; not auto-merged).

The researcher described the dot as "bottom-center to right-middle." The
detected start is bottom-LEFT, not bottom-CENTER, so this is **probably
but not certainly the same object the researcher saw.** A human review of
`outputs/video_analysis/DOD_111688809/annotated/` against the source
video at full speed should resolve it.

The dot is small (5–7 px diameter) and barely visible at normal display
scale, which matches the researcher's experience that 1 fps sampling
erased it.

### V2 (DOD_111689022) detail

What the pipeline assumes (rigid-scene homography to frame 0) breaks down
on V2 because:
- the camera tilts from sky to coastline within the 24.5 s clip, so frame 0
  shares almost no scene content with frame 200,
- the sun causes a moving lens-flare radiating from a bright source
  (visible as the radial pattern in `foreground_accumulator.png`),
- 10 fps sampling makes per-frame motion sparser.

Two visible dark dots in the sky in early frames (around (220, 110) and
(650, 210) in frame 0) match the handoff's "intermittent dot" description
but were not isolated as moving objects by the pipeline. Likely fixes for
a follow-up: per-segment stabilization, or an explicit moving-object
detector (e.g. background subtractor MOG2) instead of fixed-reference
median, or simply hand-crop the early sky-only segment and analyze in
isolation.

### V3 (DOD_111689759) detail

Camera shoots an oblique terrain panorama from a wing-mounted gimbal
(visible aircraft structure in upper-left). Pipeline ran cleanly with
zero homography failures. Foreground accumulator confirms there is
nothing moving in the scene. The 12-frame "track" the pipeline reported
is a warp-border artifact at y = 1075 (bottom edge of a 1080-tall frame).

## Pipeline gotchas worth remembering

- **Redaction rectangles are pixel-fixed in the original frame.** They
  warp under stabilization just like HUD overlays do, and produce huge
  bright/dark transitions at their leading and trailing edges. Mask them
  before stabilization.
- **HUD overlays are likewise pixel-fixed.** Same fix. A simple
  cyan/green colour gate catches the standard sensor HUD reticle, "N",
  and corner brackets.
- **Sub-pixel registration error on bright edges** (which is what these
  EO/IR scenes are full of) creates strong false foreground in a naive
  `frame − median` subtraction. Comparing each frame against a
  morphologically dilated/eroded median (i.e. against the local
  max/min) absorbs ±LOCAL_BG_RADIUS px of misregistration cheaply.
- **The two-pass approach (stabilize all → median → detect) needs the
  full stack in memory.** 668 MB for V1 (322 × 1080 × 1920). Fine for
  these clips; for longer videos, swap to disk or a sampled stack.
- **Don't trust the "dominant track" blindly.** Filter by length AND
  displacement (a dot that wanders 2 px isn't a real object), and
  always sanity-check with an annotated frame sequence.

## Implications for the project (small)

This is a detour — it does not modify schema, taxonomy, or the manifest.
But it does produce one observation worth carrying into Phase 3:

- **PURSUE Tranche 1 video items contain HUD overlays, redaction
  rectangles, AND camera motion that need careful handling for any
  automated frame-level analysis.** If the schema later supports
  per-incident video evidence with extracted trajectories, the
  preprocessing has to deal with all three. This goes into the Phase 3
  observation pile, not the schema.

## What I did NOT do (to stay low-rigor per the researcher's ask)

- No unit tests for the pipeline.
- No commit of stabilized frames to the archive (transient under `frames/`).
- No taxonomy proposal based on the V1 trajectory.
- No re-engineering of V2 to handle the sky-to-coastline tilt + lens flare
  (would be a significant follow-up).
- No human-in-the-loop classification of the V1 candidate (UAS? bird?
  vehicle? insect on the sensor? aircraft at distance?). That's for the
  researcher.
